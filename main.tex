\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ===============================================================

\begin{itemize}
    \item Hydrogen isotope transport sets fuel retention, permeation losses and tritium accountancy in fusion systems~\cite{meschini_impact_2025, meschini_modeling_2023}.
    \item Macroscopic codes (\FESTIM{}~\cite{dark_festim_2026}, \TMAP{}8~\cite{simon_moose-based_2025}, \textsc{tessim-x}~\cite{schmid_implications_2024}, \MHIMS{}~\cite{hodille_macroscopic_2015}) solve bulk diffusion and trapping on a meshed domain.
    \item Real components contain features far thinner than the component itself: grain boundaries, oxide layers, cracks, coolant channels, and the interfaces between materials.
    \item These features often set the answer, and they resist meshing: resolving them drives the cell count and the aspect ratio of the mesh.
    \item The usual responses each discard something. Homogenise the feature into an effective bulk property and it loses its own state; reduce it to an algebraic condition on the bulk traces and it loses its own transport and its inventory; couple it externally to a system code and the coupling is no longer monolithic.
    \item \KEY{Contribution: a co-dimensional description, in which the feature is a subdomain of one dimension less than the mesh, carrying its own transport equation and coupled to the bulk by a flux.}
    \item Implemented in \FESTIM{}~\cite{dark_festim_2026}, open source, released in v2.2. \VERIFY{release version and date at submission}
    \item Three applications: a 1D plug-flow model of a coolant channel coupled to a 2D/3D pipe wall; interfaces carrying their own inventory (W/Cu, Be/BeO); grain-boundary networks in Voronoi polycrystals.
    \item Relation to the companion paper: there an interface is a reactive surface with no stored inventory; here it is a subdomain with its own state. \TODO{cite the companion once it has a number}
    \item \TODO{Say plainly what the paper claims beyond capability. Candidate: the co-dimensional description is the right default for a thin feature that carries its own state, and the cases where an algebraic condition or a homogenised property suffice are narrower than current practice assumes.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}
% ===============================================================

\begin{itemize}
    \item The thin-feature problem, quantified: a grain boundary is nanometres wide inside a millimetre-scale component, an oxide layer micrometres. \TODO{numbers and citations for representative widths}
    \item Resolving such a feature with cells costs elements, aspect ratio and conditioning, and the cost is paid everywhere the feature runs, not only where it matters.
    \item \textbf{Treatment 1: homogenisation.} Fold the feature into an effective diffusivity or solubility. Loses the feature's own field, so it cannot report the inventory held in the feature or the flux carried along it, and it assumes the two phases stay locally equilibrated. \TODO{cite: Hart-type effective diffusivity, short-circuit diffusion}
    \item \textbf{Treatment 2: an algebraic interface condition.} Continuity of chemical potential imposed on the traces. Assumes instantaneous equilibration and no stored inventory, which molecular dynamics at Be/BeO~\cite{hodille_molecular_2022} and rate-equation modelling at W/Cu~\cite{silva-solis_hydrogen_2026} both contradict.
    \item \textbf{Treatment 3: external coupling.} Hand the feature to a separate 0D or 1D system model and couple by exchanged boundary data~\cite{meschini_modeling_2023}. Separate solver, separate time step, and the coupling is explicit. \TODO{cite a system code, e.g. PathSim}
    \item \KEY{A co-dimensional subdomain keeps what all three discard: its own field, its own transport terms, its own boundary conditions and exports, assembled monolithically with the bulk into a single nonlinear solve.}
    \item The mathematics is not new. Mixed-dimensional coupling is established for flow in fractured porous media and for 1D--3D vascular problems. \TODO{cite: D'Angelo \& Quarteroni; Boon, Nordbotten \& Yotov; Laurino \& Zunino; Kuchta et al.}
    \item What is new is its availability in a macroscopic hydrogen transport code, alongside trapping, multi-species reactions and the rest of the physics such a code already carries. \VERIFY{that no other H-transport code offers this}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Governing equations and coupling to the bulk}
\label{sec:method:governing}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{itemize}
    \item Setting: a domain $\Omega$ of dimension $d$, partitioned into volume subdomains, with a manifold $\Gamma$ of dimension $d-1$ embedded in it.
    \item Bulk equation, unchanged from \FESTIM{} v2~\cite{dark_festim_2026}: $\partial_t c = \nabla\cdot(D\nabla c) + S$ per subdomain, with trapping and reactions available as usual.
    \item Manifold equation: the same balance written with the tangential gradient $\nabla_\Gamma$, with its own diffusivity, and with an advection velocity projected onto $\Gamma$. \TODO{write the equation, and define $\nabla_\Gamma$}
    \item Coupling is written twice: the same exchange $J$ leaves the bulk as a flux through $\Gamma$ and enters $\Gamma$ as a source. \TODO{equation pair}
    \item $\Gamma$ may sit on the outer boundary, be buried inside one subdomain, separate two subdomains, or be adjacent to as many subdomains as a grain-boundary network has grains.
    \item The exchange law is a property of the (manifold, adjacent subdomain) pair, not of a branch of the manifold, so a network with different kinetics on each grain is expressible.
    \item The form of $J$ is the user's. A linear $J = k\,(c_\mathrm{b} - c_\Gamma)$ is the simplest; a mass-action law recovers the kinetic interface channels of the companion paper. \TODO{cite companion}
    \item \paragraph{Units} The manifold variable has no imposed convention: a volumetric concentration for a layer of thickness $\lambda$ (source $J/\lambda$), an areal density for an adsorbed layer (source $J$), or a line density. A flux boundary condition is per unit area and a source is per unit volume, so the two halves of the exchange are not dimensionally identical.
    \item \KEY{Fix one convention here and use it throughout the paper, and say which.} \TODO{decide: areal density is the natural one for the interface application, volumetric for the coolant channel. Check whether one convention covers all three applications.}
    \item Limits worth stating: a manifold with fast exchange and negligible capacity approaches an algebraic interface condition; a manifold with a thickness $\lambda$ should reproduce a resolved layer of that thickness. \TODO{verify both, analytically if possible}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Weak formulation and mixed-dimensional assembly}
\label{sec:method:weak}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{itemize}
    \item The residual cannot be written as a single form. The manifold gradient is only defined on a measure whose domain is the submesh, while the terms coupling the manifold field to the bulk field must be integrated on the parent mesh, because a bulk function cannot be resolved inside a codim-1 integral.
    \item \textsc{dolfinx}~\cite{baratta_dolfinx_2023} compiles one integration domain per form, so the residual is split into groups, one blocked form list per integration domain.
    \item The groups are accumulated into the same vector and matrix through custom nonlinear-solver callbacks, so the system stays monolithic: one nonlinear solve, one Jacobian, no operator splitting and no sub-iteration between the manifold and the bulk.
    \item An interior manifold is integrated over interior facets, where each term must be restricted to one side. Consequently a single exchange term may not read the bulk concentrations of both sides at once; one exchange is declared per adjacent subdomain.
    \item Beyond two adjacent subdomains the two-sided restriction has no meaning, since past a triple junction the network runs between a different pair of grains. Each adjacent subdomain therefore gets its own integral on which it is the positive side, and a facet shared by two grains is integrated once per grain.
    \item The manifold is tagged in the facet meshtags rather than the cell meshtags, so its identifier shares a namespace with the surface subdomains.
    \item \TODO{Decide how much of this belongs in the main text and how much in an appendix. The restriction argument is load-bearing for Application 3 and should stay.}

\end{itemize}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{\FESTIM{} user API}
\label{sec:method:api}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{itemize}
    \item A manifold is declared as a \texttt{VolumeSubdomain} whose \texttt{dim} is one less than the mesh dimension, with a \texttt{locator}. \TODO{code listing}
    \item It can be used directly wherever a surface is expected, for instance as the subdomain of a flux boundary condition, with no separate surface subdomain on the same facets.
    \item The exchange is a \texttt{ParticleFluxBC} on the bulk paired with a \texttt{ParticleSource} on the manifold, with \texttt{species\_dependent\_value} carrying both concentrations across the two meshes. \TODO{code listing}
    \item Available on a manifold: advection with an ambient velocity vector that self-projects onto $\Gamma$, drift terms, reactions, implicit species, fixed-concentration conditions on the ends of the manifold, and an outflow condition at an outlet.
    \item An end of a manifold with nothing imposed on it is a closed end, carrying zero total flux with advection balanced by back-diffusion. This is a trap for users and worth one sentence.
    \item Exports: surface fluxes and derived quantities on the manifold and on its boundary.
    \item A pair of subdomains is separated either by an algebraic interface condition or by a manifold, never both; \FESTIM{} raises if the two cover the same facets.
    \item \paragraph{Mesh caveat} The manifold is meshed by the facets of the parent mesh, so resolution along the feature is inherited from the bulk mesh. A wall needing few cells through its thickness may still need many along its length to resolve what happens on the manifold.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ===============================================================

\begin{itemize}
    \item When a co-dimensional description is the right choice: the feature is thin compared with the scale over which the bulk concentration varies, and it carries either its own state or its own transport.
    \item When it is not: when the profile across the feature matters, or when its thickness is comparable to the bulk gradient scale, in which case it should be resolved.
    \item Relation to an algebraic interface condition: the two are mutually exclusive on the same facets, and the manifold approaches the algebraic condition in the limit of fast exchange and vanishing capacity. The choice is therefore not either/or but a question of which limit the physics sits in.
    \item Relation to homogenisation: the applications give three cases where the effective-property route loses something specific and measurable.
    \item The unit convention for the manifold variable is a genuine hazard for users, and worth restating here.
    \item Resolution along the manifold is inherited from the parent mesh, which is the main practical constraint on the method.
    \item Cost, collected across the three applications. \TODO{fill in once the numbers exist}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:discussion:limitations}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{itemize}
    \item Codimension 2, a line embedded in a 3D mesh, is not covered here. \VERIFY{what the implementation supports}
    \item Parallel execution: some of the network cases are currently serial only. \VERIFY{status at submission}
    \item The exchange coefficient is the real gap. The formulation is only as good as the mass-transfer or trapping kinetics supplied to it, and for most interfaces those are not measured.
    \item Coupling to the kinetic interface channels of the companion paper, so a manifold can carry both an inventory and competing reaction channels.
    \item Adaptivity, and evolving manifolds such as a growing oxide or a propagating crack.
\end{itemize}
